UK Plug-In Solar: Where Things Actually Stand in July 2026

Since the government announced plug-in solar would be coming to UK shops "within months" back in March, the headlines have been relentlessly positive. Currys, B&Q, Amazon and Lidl have all signed up. The wiring regulations have been updated. A safety consultation has been launched and closed.

So why can't you still just walk into a shop and buy one?

This article cuts through the headlines and gives you the honest picture — what's actually happened, what hasn't, what the 22 July government response means, and what your options are right now.

What Has Actually Happened

A lot — just not quite everything needed for a fully DIY self-install kit to be on shop shelves yet. Here's the timeline of what's confirmed:

  • 15 March 2026 — Government written ministerial statement formally committing to plug-in solar legalisation
  • 24 March 2026 — Public announcement: plug-in solar available "within months," Lidl and Amazon named as retail partners
  • 15 April 2026 — BS 7671 Amendment 4 published, updating UK wiring regulations to accommodate plug-in solar connections up to 800W
  • 16 June 2026 — Major retail roundtable: Currys, B&Q, Amazon, Asda, Screwfix and Wickes join government plans; formal safety consultation launched
  • 30 June 2026 — Consultation on the interim product specification closed
  • 22 July 2026 (expected) — Government response to consultation published, confirming final policy decisions

That's genuinely significant progress in under four months. But there's one critical piece still missing.

The Missing Piece — And Why It Matters

The honest status right now is this: you still cannot legally plug a solar panel into a standard wall socket yourself.

This surprises many people who read the March and June headlines — so here's why.

There are actually two separate things required before a true DIY plug-in solar kit can go on sale:

1. Updated wiring regulations — Done. BS 7671 Amendment 4, published 15 April 2026, updated the UK's electrical rules to permit plug-in solar connections. This is the legal foundation. Without it, no manufacturer could certify a product for the UK market at all.

2. A certified product specification — Not yet finalised. The DESNZ Interim Product Specification — the technical document that defines exactly what a compliant plug-in solar kit must do — was published as a draft for consultation on 16 June 2026. The consultation closed 30 June. The government's response, confirming the final specification, is expected around 22 July 2026.

Until that specification is finalised and manufacturers certify their kits against it, no certified plug-in solar kit legally exists in the UK — regardless of what the wiring regulations say. The wiring rules create the framework; the product specification defines what's allowed to operate within it.

As Plug Solar Hub puts it plainly: "No certified plug-in solar kit yet exists, because the DESNZ Interim Product Specification has not yet published."

What the 22 July Response Will Confirm

The government's response to the consultation — expected around 22 July — is the next genuinely significant milestone. According to the official consultation document, it will confirm:

  • Final policy decisions on the interim product specification
  • Any amendments to legislation — specifically the Plugs and Sockets etc. (Safety) Regulations 1994
  • The design of the Interim Product Standard that manufacturers must certify against
  • Next steps and implementation timeline

Once that response is published, manufacturers can begin certifying their products. Once certified, kits appear on the ENA Type Test Register — and retailers can legally stock and sell them as self-install products.

The government has committed to "moving at pace" following publication. A government response to the consultation is expected around 22 July 2026, with decisions on next steps and implementation to follow as soon as practicable. Realistically, most UK households will be able to walk into a shop or order online and buy a certified plug-in solar kit from late 2026 onwards.

What You Can Do Right Now — Legally

The fact that the DIY self-install route isn't yet available doesn't mean you're locked out of plug-in solar entirely. There is one fully legal route available today:

Buy a kit and have a qualified electrician make the connection.

A CPS-registered electrician (registered with NICEIC, NAPIT, or ELECSA) can connect an 800W plug-in solar kit via a hardwired fused spur — exactly the same method used for any other small-scale generation system. This is fully compliant with BS 7671 Amendment 4 and all current UK regulations. You still notify your DNO under G98 within 28 days, but the electrician typically handles this as part of the job.

The typical cost for the electrician connection is £150–£300 on top of the kit price. The savings and performance of the system are identical to a self-installed kit — the only difference is the connection method and the upfront cost. At £100–£180 per year in savings, you typically recover the electrician cost within 1–2 years of operation.

EcoFlow's STREAM 800W kit is currently available on Amazon UK from around £499, and is widely considered the most likely product to receive early UK certification given EcoFlow's position as the government's named technology partner for the rollout. Jackery is another well-regarded option with a strong UK presence and UKCA-marked products.

A Note on the Headlines vs. Reality

It's worth being direct about something: some of the coverage around the March and June announcements overstated where things stood. The government's political announcements moved faster than the technical standards process — which is not unusual, but has led to confusion among consumers who read "plug-in solar is coming to Lidl" and went looking for a kit to buy.

The direction of travel is real and the timeline is moving. But "the government has announced it" and "you can buy one today" are different things, and it's worth understanding that distinction before you start making purchasing decisions.

The good news is that the remaining gap is genuinely small — a government response document and a manufacturer certification process, not a fundamental rethink. Late 2026 for widespread retail availability remains a realistic expectation.

What to Watch For

These are the specific milestones that will trigger the next meaningful changes:

  • ~22 July 2026 — Government consultation response published; final product specification confirmed
  • Summer/Autumn 2026 — First certified kits listed on ENA Type Test Register; retailers begin stocking
  • Late 2026 — Widespread retail availability; Lidl, B&Q, Currys and Amazon expected to stock certified kits
  • 2027 — Simplified G98 notification process expected; potential Smart Export Guarantee access for plug-in systems
